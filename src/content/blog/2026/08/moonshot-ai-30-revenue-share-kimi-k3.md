Artificial Intelligence startup **Moonshot AI** has reportedly sent shockwaves through the tech industry by demanding a **30% revenue share** from major cloud providers—specifically Microsoft, Amazon Web Services (AWS), and Google Cloud—to deploy its upcoming flagship model, **Kimi K3**. 

This bold move challenges the established dynamic where cloud giants charge API fees, introducing a model similar to Apple's App Store revenue split.

## A Shift in the AI Value Chain

Traditionally, AI developers pay cloud infrastructure providers for compute resources, or cloud platforms resell model APIs to enterprise customers, keeping a small distribution margin. Moonshot AI's demand flips this hierarchy:

1. **Compute Subsidy:** Moonshot AI wants compute credits offset by future shared revenue.
2. **Distribution Premium:** Since Kimi K3 has shown breakout reasoning performance in Chinese languages, US cloud providers want to offer it to their enterprise clients.
3. **App Store Model:** A 30% cut of all subscription fees and API queries run on Cloud infrastructure.

> "The reasoning models of 2026 are extremely expensive to train but provide unmatched business value. We believe a revenue-share model aligns our incentives with cloud platforms," a source close to Moonshot AI remarked.

## The Clouds' Dilemma

Cloud providers are in a tight spot. On one hand, hosting high-demand models drives enterprise cloud consumption (data storage, pipelines, hosting). On the other hand, yielding a 30% revenue split sets a costly precedent that OpenAI, Anthropic, or Meta might follow.
